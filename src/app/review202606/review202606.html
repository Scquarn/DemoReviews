Next Fest June

All told her about the warmth of the first fire which was strong enough to overcome the cold. The day she finally approached it the host grinned wide but there was no other greeting or even the slightest of nods to acknowledge her. She was just one more soul adding to the crowd.
Whenever the warmth lessened the host brought fresh fuel and they threw it on the pyre together. It was named the feeding and not a single was ever missed. She thanked him and fully embraced his soothing gift that gave her a place to rest.
Time went on but the warmth stayed and she began to forget the cold that had always been there. Stoking the fire became routine. It had to be done.
No matter how many they became, the warmth spread to all. With the cold slowly weakening the host’s grin widened and it never once wavered. Not even when he brought the child to throw on the pyre. It cried and begged but on the pyre it landed all the same and nobody questioned it. The fire needed fuel and it was their routine to stoke the flames with whatever the host brought, this was simply how things went. Fuel went on the pyre and they did it together for not a single feeding could be missed. The flames were ever hungry and the host always brought more innocents to burn. Despite that she still held out her hands towards the warmth that came from the smouldering remains. It was unjust, it was wrong but she didn’t want to feel the cold again. The fire had to survive.
As time slipped away and the bone pile grew. Became as tall as a mountain, fell over, rose again, spread far and further. Warmth everywhere, warmth for all. What once was frozen land became part of the pyre and the warmth increased with every feeding. Became hot, turned scorching.
For a long time she never had doubts but then the feeding was interrupted by pure chance. Fuel brought to burn had fallen off the pyre. It was only for a moment before it was pushed back in the flames but it was enough. For that split second the warmth faltered, the temperature lessened a fraction. A change so small, yet enough to remind her of the past, of the long forgotten cold. She remembered how it felt not to burn, how it was to breathe fresh air, how silent the world had been and how snow had fallen endlessly from the hollow grey sky and she realised that she longed for it. She wanted to feel the touch of frost on her skin. A frozen kiss, just one more time.
It made her turn away from the heat, made her search for the cold eternal but no matter where she went the pyre had already spread. The land was taken by ash and the air filled with smoke. Desperate she turned back and looked for the host, to ask him if there was still a place where the cold remained but she could not find him. The pyre burned alone and the next feeding came but he wasn’t there to bring new fuel. The people turned to her and asked what to do but she had no answer for them.
Again she felt the warmth wavering and now the people also felt it but for them it didn’t create longing and instead brought them terror. They had heard whispers of the freezing, the world savage. The fear connected them together like invisible threads of a giant web and that single pull, that tiny shift in the temperature, it was enough to alert all of them. A new feeding began but this time without the host’s guidance and whatever they could find was thrown on the pyre, including themselves. Everything to the flames in hope they wouldn’t fade but nothing helped.
Scorching turned to heat, turned to warmth and finally the fire died and left behind neither warmth nor cold but the empty ever dark.
In the last shimmers of the fire she saw the host laying at a far away arm of the pyre, the everlasting grin still on his lips. He was dead and with the pyre reduced to it’s last glimmers she made a choice. Taking one of the sparks in her hands she understood the reason for why it had burned until now and with the shadows approaching only two things remained: Screams of those lost in the dark and a wide grin on lips illuminated by the last light.

Demos

It has come: Next Fest. No doubt about it as this blog has reappeared before you. This assembly of words and thoughts mad. Like a comet it graces your orbit and may it only mirror Halley: Rare but ever reoccurring. I do consider that it’s late, but it does exist and it’s here. So enjoy this little pile.

1

Our first climb in this Next Fest will be with Ascenders: Beyond the Peak. Here we guide a team of three climbers in a turn-based exploration roguelite with survival elements sprinkled in. On paper it sounds interesting and also the screenshots on the steam page look good but after trying it out I found it rather meh.
Don't misunderstand, the mechanics are well done. You are climbing with a team of three, where each member has a different skillset that helps to survive the harsh climb and the whole setup reminds of other similar team roguelites like darkest dungeon. Each turn you have to decide between different actions like collecting berries for food, climbing the wall, evade rocks falling from above and other similar stuff. Between each climbing level you arrive at camp where you can level and upgrade your team, eat food and upgrade the camp. Found items have to be puzzled in the backpack Tetris style. On top of all that there are also day and night mechanics, different terrains, and lots more. There is a lot in here but I don't vibe with it. Nothing is out of place but also nothing is in the right place to make me interested to continue the climb. Demo is still on steam, better take a look yourself for this one, but for me it's a no.

Gameplay feels alright but there is a lot and I mean a lot of talking. Which is okay normally, dialogue has it's place, but what about murder? I mean this is hotline Miami copy.... The opening Cinematic is literally just walking forwards for a few minutes. Nothing else happens. Why is it even here? Sometimes people make weird choices when looking back at their work through rose colored glasses. Caught by the way, I might have done the same before.
The real story besides walking in a line is told in comic strips. An alright style I guess? I rate this furry out of Hotline Miami with boring dialogue that you want to skip past to murder some dudes.

Talking about flashgames, let's take a look at GemCraft - Legacy Collection. From a shitty flashgame like to an actual former flashgame. Seems like there are a few of them coming to steam recently? Saw happy wheels recently too. Anyway, this is GemCraft. One of the original Tower Defences. If you have played this before then you already know this is good but for all the zoomers that don't know the OG let's explain it quickly: This is the classic TD experience. Each level has a set path the monsters walk to your base and you have to create towers to defend against them but the unique spin in this game is that towers are rather cheap and can’t shoot because the real focus are the gems that you place inside them. Each gem can be upgraded by combining two of the same tier. There are about eight total different gems, from some that deal high damage, have high range, poison the enemy, slow, have splash damage and some others. The fun part is that any type of gem can be combined together with others, but they must have the same tier. This means you can create some cool combinations like a slowing splash, a high range damage gem, whatever you can come up with. The game series is a damn classic and this combines all three, GemCraft 1, Gemcraft 0 and GemCraft Labyrinth into a single Collection. Go play the demo if you have never played one of these as they are a lot of fun. Keeping an eye on this one, maybe getting it if it's cheap.

From strategic tower placement to actual strategy in ZeroSpace, a new RTS game. I open the game and see there are three modes: campaign, galactic war and versus. Let's check out the campaign for now. A cutscene starts and story is really basic: Humans use Flux for FTL, humans want more flux, the aliens have the flux, you know what happens next. On the first mission we crash on an alien planet with our small task force and have to fight along a few waypoints to get more units and to defend the aliens who look a bit like plants at times. During this I notice that the dialogue in this game has some fun lines so I'm going to include some in this review, like this one, "We are up shits creek without a paddle". Finish the mission to find some weird super alien that we collect and get promoted for. No clue what it is, highly classified you know the drill. Story is kinda basic to be honest but it's quite fun. Actual gameplay feels nice too. Controls are smooth and the classic RTS mechanics are all here, so it checks all the boxes. Between the missions you can walk around the ship and talk to the other members in your crew and the dialogue with them is well made too. "A cesspool of incompetence and nepotism but we are all swimming in it together." There is still a bit of scuff, like cutscenes getting cut off or the UI being a bit rough at places but overall it's a decently made game. I didn't try the other modes much because I tired galactic war but didn't understand how the mode worked as the tutorial didn't explain anything at all. The chat was spammed in Chinese, so there's that.

The others talk to you through thought communication or some shit. You have no idea what you are doing, why you are here, what's going on or anything really.
The kobolds are just two hits to kills then you continue to walk. This might be purgatory. I mean it. You have to listen to these people who talk as if you care deeply for them while continuing along the same ruin assets over and over again. It's so bad and the only reason why I endure it is to see if these idiots die painfully and finally shut the fuck up.
Combat tries to be souls like, but who even cares at this point. This game sucks all the balls, if the rest of this game is as bad as the intro then they could use this to torture people. Seriously. Same with closing the fucking thing. Fun fact: the game doesn't pause during this. Yes, this is hell on earth. Ahoi matey and welcome to the Corsair Cove. A pirate city builder. The loading screen seems infinite but after around five minutes the game starts. Our crew loses their ship and we have to start over by building a new pirate settlement on a small island. The graphics are awesome, rich blue water, lush greenery, a giant skull shaped mountain in the centre of the island. In short, it looks damn beautiful.
It's a fun city builder as everything feels very organic. You place down buildings and connect them together with paths, seems normal at first but then you realise that most of the building happens vertically and the paths turn into wooden bridges, ladders and suspension bridges. The further you build upwards on the island the more paths get created, almost like inside an anthill. You have to satisfy the classic needs like food, stone, wood, etc. but there are also more unique mechanics. If people get to unhappy they will mutineer and if it gets too low the game is lost. There are also open sea battles that get fought with the ships you build. The battles are turn based and similar to classic roguelike card games, but it seems here we get set decks for them depending on the ship you have built. Overall the game is great, a city builder that convinces for a change. Good game.

From pirates to dungeoneers. Dungeon Settlers isn't a city builder but a colony simulator. Think rimworld or dwarf fortress. Starting the game I see a notice that says if I encounter a critical bug I should restart. Well not a terrible idea that. Gotta tell people that don't know... Perhaps? Why is this here?
Despite this being a dungeon settlement management game I spent all tutorial learning how to control units. There are thousands of menus and buttons to click. Everything is quite complicated and unintuitive. If you don't listen to and remember the tutorial you will be quite lost.
Maybe there's a cool game here but to me this is kinda boring. Too bad I didn't even get to the settlement part, which seems to be the core mechanics of this game but with the combat being very basic despite all these buttons and menus you have to navigate and click I just couldn't take it any longer. Let's visit space in Hull Rupture. At first I thought this was a game like FTL: Faster than light but this is actually a tower defence and it's made by the makers of despot's game which was already quite interesting.
We build a spaceship, manage different resources like people in your crew, energy resources, etc. and we build towers on the outside of the ship to defend against incoming waves. It's a bit of a mix between tower defence, city builder and roguelite. We also get different artifacts and random shops after each wave. So everything you expect pretty much. It's really fun and trying to get the ship up and running to fit higher tier rooms and towers is a lot of fun. I'll wishlist this one as the demo makes me want to keep playing for another round even after I have already done quite a few.

To truly judge this game however we also need to see the other half of the game, but if they make it with the same quality this could be a really cool game. ReStory: Chill Electronic Repairs is a job simulator. Of course employment is illustrated as something cozy here. Damn propaganda. Fun aside, in this you own a repair shop and have to take apart old or malfunctioning electronics to repair them. There is no time limit or challenge, just the comfy work you do day to day. There seems to be an overarching story here too. It's fun for a while but I don’t know, it's missing something exciting for me to keep going. Not a bad game however. It's well made and I can see the appeal. Steam reviews are super positive too, so maybe check it out.
